I am a Robotics Software Engineer on the motion planning team at General Motors, where I build ROS 2 motion planning and perception software for UR and FANUC collaborative robots running on NVIDIA Jetson Thor. My work spans collision-aware planning with MoveIt 2, OMPL, Pilz and NVIDIA cuRobo, real-time planning scene mapping from depth cameras, vision-guided pick-and-place driven by grasp poses from learned segmentation and grasping models, and the tooling that lets new robot cells be configured in minutes.

I hold a Ph.D. in Mechanical Engineering from the [Mechatronics, Controls and Robotics Laboratory (MCRL)](https://mechatronics.engineering.nyu.edu/) at NYU Tandon School of Engineering. My dissertation, *Exploring Extended Reality Technologies for Human-Centered Interaction: From Rehabilitation to Human-Robot Collaboration*, developed head-mounted and handheld virtual and mixed reality interfaces, combined with perception algorithms and supplementary sensors, so that anyone, regardless of technical background, can perform VR exergames for upper-limb rehabilitation or work with robots intuitively.

Background & Motivation
======
I began my engineering journey at [Tecnológico de Monterrey](https://tec.mx/en/estado-de-mexico-campus), where I earned a scholarship to study Mechatronics Engineering and designed a camera-guided robotic arm for my senior capstone project. After graduation, I worked as an automation engineer in the oil and gas sector, refining my skills in PLC programming, HMI and SCADA design, sensor integration, and real-time control systems. I also founded Heroeng, a company that repaired mechanical seals for the oil industry. These experiences solidified my passion for seamless human–machine collaboration and laid the groundwork for my transition to robotics research.

Path to Research
======
In 2018, I enrolled in NYU’s M.S. in Mechatronics & Robotics program, where I explored machine-vision and learning-algorithm projects. In November 2018, I began volunteering as a research assistant in two labs: the [Artificial Intelligence for Civil Engineering Laboratory (AI4CE Lab)](https://ai4ce.github.io/) led by Professor [Chen Feng](https://engineering.nyu.edu/faculty/chen-feng), and the [Applied Dynamics and Optimization Laboratory](https://wp.nyu.edu/adol/) led by Professor [Joo H. Kim](https://engineering.nyu.edu/faculty/joo-h-kim). Working on research problems in both labs inspired me to deepen my research engagement.

I then joined NYU’s Ph.D. program, advised by Professor [Vikram Kapila](https://engineering.nyu.edu/faculty/vikram-kapila), where I developed a tele-dialysis interface during the COVID-19 response, VR exergames for upper-limb rehabilitation, and handheld mixed-reality frameworks for communicating force intent and goals to robot manipulators. In 2022, I interned with Apple’s Video Engineering team as a GEM Fellow.

AI-driven perception has been a common thread through this work: from a 3D-CNN video model at Apple, to a YOLO11 instance segmentation model that I trained on synthetic data and deployed on an iPad to drive a [mixed-reality robot interface](/projects/project-3), to motion planning built around learned grasp perception at GM.

Today, I bring that human-centered research background to collaborative robotics, building practical, technically robust motion planning and perception systems for the next generation of robotic applications.
